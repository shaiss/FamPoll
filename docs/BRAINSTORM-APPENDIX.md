# Brainstorm appendix

Supporting material behind [BRAINSTORM.md](BRAINSTORM.md): the four concept directions that were explored, what the critics said, the landscape scan, and the gap check that shaped the final brief.

## The four concept directions

### Whittle

Angle: simplest thing that works.

Whittle is a one-page web app where a family opens a Trip, asks questions with plain-text options, and taps to vote. When the field needs narrowing, the organizer hits one button, Whittle, which cuts the bottom half and opens a fresh round; when it's settled, they hit Lock. The Trip page is the running ledger: Decided on top, Voting now below, every locked answer with its round trail underneath. It lives inside the family's existing Messenger thread as a link; it replaces the polls, not the chat.

**Differentiator.** The Whittle button. Rounds are not a config screen or a wizard; they are one tap that halves the field and resets the votes. Messenger polls are one-shot, buried in scroll, and ungroupable. Whittle's Trip page is a permanent scoreboard: "Friday dinner -> Taco Palace (round 2 of 2)". You open one link and see everything the family has decided, is deciding, and how it got there.

**Core loop.** 1. Organizer opens Whittle, types a trip name ("Fall break 2026"), taps Create, taps Copy link, pastes it into the existing Messenger family thread. 2. Each family member taps the link, signs in with Google once (subsequent taps skip straight in), and is auto-joined to the trip. 3. Anyone asks a question inline on the Trip page: a title ("Where do we eat Friday?") plus options, one per line. 4. Everyone taps their pick; the option shows live count and avatars; a "3 of 5 voted" line applies the nag pressure. 5. Organizer taps Whittle (bottom half cut, round 2 opens with survivors, votes reset, everyone re-taps) or Lock (leader wins; on a tie the organizer taps the winner). 6. The locked answer moves to the Decided section at the top of the Trip page with its round trail collapsed underneath; the next question is asked on the same page.

**Rounds model.** A Question has one or more Rounds; only the latest round is open. A Round is a fresh single-choice vote over a subset of the previous round's options; votes never carry across rounds, which is what makes it a round rather than a running tally. States: Question.status is 'open' or 'decided'; Round is 'current' (closed_at null) or 'closed'. Advancing: the trip creator (any question) or the question's creator (own question) sees two buttons under an open question. Whittle: rank alive options by vote count in the current round, keep the top ceil(n/2), and also keep any option tied with the last survivor (never eliminate a tie); set eliminated_round on the rest; close the round; open round n+1 with zero votes. If the rule would keep everything (all tied) or leave exactly one, the Whittle button is disabled with an inline hint and only Lock is offered. Lock: closes the current round and sets decided_option_id to the current leader; if the lead is tied, Lock becomes 'tap the winner' so the organizer picks among the tied options. Ties are always resolved by a human tap, never by the app. No deadlines, no quorum, no minimum voters: a family of five nags each other in chat and the 'N of M voted' line is the only pressure. Decided questions display the winner plus a collapsed trail: 'R1: 6 options, 5 votes -> R2: 3 options, 5 votes -> Taco Palace'. Nothing is reopenable in MVP; ask again if the family changes its mind.

**Auth model.** Google sign-in only, via Auth.js v5 with the Google provider and basic profile scope. Why Google first: no app review for basic scopes, works on every Android and every Gmail user, kids can have Family Link Google accounts, setup is a 15-minute OAuth client, and it needs no paid developer account. Apple is v1 (only if an iPhone-only relative has no Google account); Facebook is 'later' because Facebook Login requires app review, a privacy policy URL, and a data-deletion callback, and the family's Messenger habit does not imply Facebook auth. Invite flow: a Trip has a 10-character unguessable slug; opening /t/<slug> while signed in inserts a TripMember row, and unsigned visitors get the Google button with a return-to. No email invites, no approval, no removal in MVP; the link is the invite and family is the threat model. Roles: exactly two implicit ones, trip creator (can Whittle/Lock any question, delete any zero-vote question) and member (vote, ask, Whittle/Lock own questions). Kids: those with a Google account (including Family Link accounts) sign in like anyone else; kids without one do not vote in the app in week one and a parent relays their pick, which is how it works verbally today. Guests: anyone with the link is a member; there is no guest tier.

**MVP features.**

- Google sign-in (only): Hard requirement #4. One provider, one button, no email or password fields anywhere.
- Create Trip + unguessable share link: Hard requirement #1 (grouping). The link IS the invite; no invite emails, no member management.
- Auto-join on link open: Zero-friction membership for family; a signed-in visit to /t/<slug> inserts a TripMember row and that's it.
- Ask a question with plain-text options (2-10): The poll itself. Text options cover every example the founder gave: places, restaurants, destinations, '3 nights / 4 nights / 5 nights'. No date pickers, no images, no poll types.
- One-tap single-choice vote, changeable until the round closes: Messenger parity. Single choice is the simplest thing that makes a runoff meaningful.
- Live counts + voter avatars + 'N of M voted': Messenger shows names; the family expects it. The unvoted count is the only notification system in the MVP.
- Whittle button (open next round with top half; ties at the cut line survive): Hard requirement #3. This is the smallest thing that still counts as a round: a subset of options and a fresh vote.
- Lock button (declare winner; organizer picks on tie): Rounds have to end. Ties are common with 5 voters, so the tiebreak is a human tap, not an algorithm.
- Decided section at top of Trip page + collapsed round trail per question: Hard requirement #2 (keep track along the way). This is the whole reason to leave Messenger.
- Delete question while it has zero votes: Typos happen on day one and non-devs can't fix the DB. One action, one guard.
- Copy link / native share sheet: Distribution goes through the Messenger thread the family already has; Whittle never tries to be the chat.
- Web manifest for add-to-home-screen: About 20 lines; makes it feel like an app on every phone without a native build.
- 10-second polling refresh on the Trip page: Good enough for one family; avoids websockets and a realtime provider.

**Risks.**

- The family keeps using Messenger polls because the poll sits where the conversation is. Mitigation: Whittle is link-first and never tries to be the chat; if the link is not pasted into Messenger within the first week, the product is not working.
- Tiny electorates tie constantly. Cut-the-bottom-half with 5 voters and 3 options often removes nothing; the disabled-Whittle-plus-Lock path must be obvious or the organizer feels stuck.
- Google-only excludes an iPhone-only relative with no Gmail; measured on day one against the actual family roster. Apple sign-in is the v1 answer, not a launch blocker.
- No notifications means the organizer nags manually. Accepted for week one since they already nag in Messenger; if nagging fatigue shows up, web push jumps to the front of v1.
- Neon free tier sleeps; first load after idle can take 2-3 seconds, which reads as broken on a phone. Mitigation: a loading state on the Trip page or a paid tier at a few dollars a month.
- Anyone with the link joins and votes. Fine for a family, catastrophic if the link is posted publicly. There is no member removal in MVP.
- Scope creep toward trip planning (dates, itineraries, budgets). The founder said polls; the two-weekend budget is the guard.
- Votes reset every round may surprise people who expected a running tally. The round badge and 'votes reset' toast on Whittle need to make this explicit.

**Critique: Family usability skeptic.** Scores out of 10: family usability 5, feasibility 7, differentiation 6, rounds clarity 5, overall 5.

Must fix:

- Harden the state machine with four cheap moves: partial unique index ON round(question_id) WHERE closed_at IS NULL; Whittle/Lock UPDATE ... - Open Whittle/Lock to any member (family is the threat model) and drop the lurker-inflated 'N of M' in favor of 'N voted' plus avatars, or count M as members who voted on any of the trip's last three questions.

- The single Whittle tap with a 'cuts 3 of 6' preview, computed client-side from the same pure function the server executes.
- eliminated_round integer on Option instead of a round-x-option join table; alive in round N = (null OR >= N).
- Vote PK (round_id, user_id) with upsert: changeable votes and per-round reset with zero extra state or code.
- Link-is-the-invite auto-join with no invites, approvals, or member admin.
- Ties are always resolved by a human tap; the app never coin-flips.
- Delete-only-while-zero-votes as the sole edit affordance in the MVP; every other edit case becomes 'ask again'.
- The Decided ledger line plus a collapsed round trail string derived from rounds and votes, never stored.
- Feature tiers where every line carries a one-sentence reason and an explicit 'not in the founder's words' tag.
- The one-week 'was the link pasted into Messenger' kill criterion.
- Google-first because a PWA is exempt from Apple's guideline 4.8; Apple deferred until an iPhone-only relative without Gmail actually appears on the roster.

### Show of Hands

Angle: the family’s lived experience.

Show of Hands turns a family trip into a "table" with a running list of decisions. The organizer asks one question at a time ("Where?", "How long?", "Dinner tonight?"), drops one link into the Messenger group, and everyone from Grandma to the 8-year-old votes with a single tap and no account. When a vote needs narrowing it runs another round; when it settles, it gets stamped into the trip's Decision Log, so the trip page becomes the plan nobody had to write.

**Differentiator.** Seats, not accounts. A family is a roster of named seats (Mom, Dad, Maya 15, Eli 8, Grandma); a seat votes by tapping a link, never by signing in, and a kid's seat is "held" by a parent who votes with them on the parent's phone. Only the organizer ever logs in. Messenger polls can't do this because every voter must be a Facebook user, votes vanish up the chat, and there are no rounds; here every closed vote becomes a line in the trip's Decision Log with its full round history, and the reveal is a moment (avatars pile onto the winning card, coin-flip for ties) rather than a chore.

**Core loop.** 1) Dana opens the trip's Table (or taps the standing 'Tonight' table for dinner) and hits Ask. 2) She types a prompt, pastes or taps out options (emoji/photos optional), picks single-choice or ranked, chooses 'hide results until close', optionally sets a close time; defaults are pre-filled from a template (Where -> How long -> Where to stay -> What to do -> Where to eat). 3) She taps Share; the app hands one link to Messenger with an OG preview card showing the question and 'Tap to vote'. Each family seat already has a cookie from the one-time family-link claim, so the link opens straight onto that person's ballot; kid seats appear as a 'Vote as Eli' toggle on the parent's ballot. 4) Everyone taps a card and is done: confetti, avatar drops onto their choice (or a sealed envelope if results are hidden), 'Back to Messenger'. Dana's Table shows '4 of 6 in' with the missing faces so she can poke them in chat. 5) The round closes on deadline, on full quorum, or when Dana taps Close. If one option has a majority it Locks; otherwise Dana gets a one-tap 'Runoff top 2' (or 'Parent's call' / 'Coin flip' on ties) and the next round ships the same way. 6) The locked result is stamped into the Decision Log on the Table with a 'Post result to chat' card, and the next queued question is suggested. Repeat until the trip is planned; during the trip, the Table is the reference.

**Rounds model.** Question states: draft -> open (round n) -> closed (round n) -> either open (round n+1) or locked -> archived. Each round is its own ballot with its own option set, close time, and votes; earlier rounds are preserved and expandable from the Decision Log. Who advances: the question owner (the host, or any seat with role adult marked as co-host for that table). Advancing options after a close: 'Runoff top 2' (carries the top two by votes, plus any tied for second), 'Runoff top N' (owner picks N), 'Reopen same options' (for low turnout), 'Lock winner', or on a tie 'Parent's call' (owner picks, logged as 'Locked by Dana, 3-3 tie') or 'Coin flip' (server-side random, animated, logged). Auto rules per question: if auto-close is set and the leader has more than 50% of cast ballots, it locks automatically; otherwise it closes and waits for the owner's one-tap runoff. Ties with 'Runoff' selected auto-carry all tied options. Deadlines are optional; trip templates default to 48 hours for planning questions and 45 minutes for the 'Tonight' table. Quorum: each seat is marked required or optional (kids default optional, guests optional); 'close when everyone required has voted' is a per-question toggle and is the default for the Tonight table. Abstain counts toward quorum but not toward majority. Vetoes ('Not that') never remove an option automatically in v1; they surface as a red flag count on the option so the owner sees 'Maya and Lou both said not that' before choosing a runoff. Ranked-choice rounds (v1) run instant-runoff inside a single round; the log shows the elimination sequence as if it were rounds. Sealed results stay hidden per round until that round closes; the organizer sees only who has voted, not what.

**Auth model.** Two tiers. Hosts sign in with Google, Apple, or Facebook (Auth.js, plus an email magic-link fallback, matching the existing Login mock); a host creates the Family and its Seats by first name and emoji/avatar. Everyone else is a Seat, not a user: the host shares one family link into the Messenger group; each person taps it, picks their name from the roster (with a photo hint), and the browser is stamped with a long-lived, revocable seat token in an HttpOnly cookie. From then on any poll link opens straight to their ballot. Each seat also has a personal link the host can DM as a fallback (cookie lost, new phone) and rotate with one tap if it leaks. Any seat holder can optionally 'claim' their seat with a social login later to carry it across devices; this is never required. Kids under 13: no token of their own; their seat is marked 'helped by' a parent seat, and on the parent's ballot a 'Vote as Eli' toggle opens the picture-first kid ballot; votes are stored as seat=Eli, cast_by=Dana. Kid seats store first name and avatar only. Teens can have their own seat link (parent's choice) or be helped. Guests: a per-table join link creates a single-table seat from a typed name, no roster membership, no visibility into other tables, deleted when the table archives. Wrong-person taps: every ballot header shows 'Voting as Lou. Not you?' which clears the cookie and returns to the roster picker. The wrong-seat risk is mitigated by hiding results (nothing to spy on), by rotating personal links, and by the log showing cast_by for proxies.

**MVP features.**

- Tables (events) with a Decision Log timeline: Requirement 1 and 2: polls grouped by trip, and the Table page is the record of what got decided, in order, with round history behind each entry.
- Seats roster with account-free voting via signed seat links: The friction killer. Teens and grandparents never sign in; one family link claims a seat once, then every poll link just works. Requirement 4 satisfied for the organizer only, which is all that's needed.
- Organizer sign-in with Google, Apple, Facebook (plus email magic link fallback): Matches the existing Login mock and the founder's ask; Facebook because the family already lives in Messenger. Only the host and anyone who chooses to claim a seat across devices ever hits this screen.
- Proxy (helped-by) voting and the picture-first kid ballot: Kids under 10 are half the fun and half the headcount; without a proxy mode their votes are either faked by a parent or skipped.
- Rounds: manual advance, auto-runoff of top N, lock on majority: Requirement 3. Most questions are one round, so rounds stay invisible until a result fails to reach majority; then the runoff is one tap.
- Sealed results (hidden until close) as the default for trip questions: Stops the visible-leader bandwagon and lets the teen vote against the parents without a fight in the chat.
- Who's in / who's missing view with Messenger-friendly nudge text: Nagging stays human and inside Messenger; the app never sends reminders to family members, which is what makes people mute apps.
- Share to Messenger with OG preview card and 'Post result to chat' card: The app fits around Messenger instead of replacing it. The link IS the invite; the result card closes the loop without anyone opening the app.
- Big-text / one-question-per-screen ballot that respects OS font scaling: Grandparents are the persona most likely to quietly stop voting. This is a design constraint, not a setting.

**Risks.**

- Seat links forwarded in the group chat let one person vote as another; mitigated by sealed results, per-seat link rotation, the 'Not you?' switch, and proxy attribution, but a determined sibling can still cheat a dinner vote.
- Cookie loss (private browsing, Messenger's in-app browser clearing storage, new phone) reintroduces the 'who are you' screen and erodes the three-second promise; Messenger's in-app webview behavior on iOS needs real testing.
- Facebook Login requires Meta app review, a privacy policy, and data-deletion callbacks; Apple Sign In has its own review and relay-email quirks; either could delay the MVP by weeks if started late.
- Kid seats touch COPPA-adjacent territory; keeping kids as PII-free seats with no tokens is the design, but any drift toward kid accounts or push must be checked.
- Rounds could bloat the creation flow; if the Ask sheet ever needs more than one round-related decision up front, adoption dies with the organizer.
- Low quorum from teens is the most likely failure of the loop; deadlines and 'close anyway' are the fix, but if Dana ends up voting for everyone the log becomes fiction.
- Only one person per family ever really 'uses' the app, so growth is word of mouth between organizers; there's no viral loop through voters unless the result card in Messenger carries the brand well.
- The existing Login mock asks invitees to sign in with a social account; this concept deliberately reverses that for family members, and if the founder disagrees the friction thesis collapses.

### Whittle

Angle: the decision engine.

Whittle turns every family question into a ladder the family climbs together: pile up ideas, shortlist the favorites, pick one — each rung a round with a deadline that closes itself the moment the last person votes. Rounds hang off an event (the trip, Nana's 80th, Friday dinner), so the event page is both the to-do list of what is still open and the receipt of everything already decided. Login is one tap with Google, Apple or Facebook; kids vote from a parent's phone with a profile and a PIN.

**Differentiator.** The Ladder with receipts. A Messenger poll is one flat question that scrolls away; a Whittle decision is Gather -> Shortlist -> Final with tie rules a 10-year-old can recite ("tie? we revote between the tied ones; still tied? the app flips a coin"), auto-closing when everyone has voted, and every rung is kept: "Lisbon beat Porto 5-1 in the final, Jun 2 · reopened once by Dana." The trip page reads as a running record of how the family decided, not just what.

**Core loop.** 1) Organizer creates an event from a template (Trip, Party, Weekly dinner, Blank); it comes with an ordered ladder of decisions (Destination -> Dates -> Lodging -> Getting there) and an invite link to paste into Messenger. 2) The first round opens (Gather): everyone adds ideas and taps 'Done adding'; the round closes at the deadline or the moment everyone is done. 3) The engine advances: Shortlist opens with the gathered pile, everyone checks their favorites or skips; top N advance (ties at the cut all advance). 4) Final opens: 2 options = pick one, 3+ = rank them; the last vote starts a 60-second lock timer, then the result is frozen with a 'how we got here' explainer and a ledger entry, and the next dependent decision auto-opens. 5) During the event, 'quick votes' (single round, hours-long deadline) get pinned to a day: tonight's dinner, tomorrow's beach. 6) After the event, the page becomes the record: every decision, how it was won, who nudged what; the summary is shareable as text or link.

**Rounds model.** DEFINITION. A Round is one bounded pass of participation over a frozen option set: {type, method, input options, close condition, rule, output}. Output is either a narrowed option set handed to the next round or a winner that decides the Decision. A Decision is an ordered list of rounds (its 'ladder'); at most one round of a decision is open at a time. Every round has a per-member participation state: none | submitted | skipped. Ballots are editable until the round locks.

ROUND TYPES (kid explanation in quotes). Gather / method=ideas: everyone adds options; 'Done adding' = participation. 'Pile up every idea.' Shortlist / method=approval: check any number of options (organizer may cap at K). Top N advance (default N=3). 'Tick everything you'd be happy with.' Final / method=single when 2 options, ranked (instant runoff) when 3+; organizer may switch 3+ to single ('just pick one'). 'Put your favorites in order; if your first one gets knocked out, your vote jumps to your next one.' Check / method=yesno on a single proposal. 'Thumbs up or down.' Quick vote = a Decision whose ladder is a single Final with organizer-supplied options and an hours-long deadline.

STATES. Decision: planned -> active -> decided; plus needs_organizer, stale (decided but an upstream decision was reopened), cancelled. Round: draft -> open -> closing -> closed; voided (kept in history, never deleted). Closed rounds carry an outcome: narrowed | winner | tied | no_quorum | empty.

TRANSITIONS (actor in brackets). draft->open [organizer Start, or engine auto-start when previous round closes with auto_advance=on (default) or when depends_on decision becomes decided]. Options freeze at open, except Gather. open->open [organizer Extend (closes_at moves later, ballots kept); organizer Withdraw option with reason (approvals for it dropped, rankings compact, single-choice votes for it become 'needs re-pick' notifications); organizer Veto option with reason before open or during Gather only]. open->closing [engine, when participation (submitted+skipped) == eligible and early_close=on (default on): a 60-second grace window during which anyone may still edit; if someone clears their ballot during grace, back to open]. open|closing->closed [engine at closes_at (idempotent cron, row-locked); organizer Close (allowed only if quorum met); organizer Close-short (quorum not met, requires confirmation, logged as such)]. closed->next round open|draft [engine Advance when outcome=narrowed; draft instead of open if decision.auto_advance=off, showing 'Up next: Final, waiting for Dana to start']. closed->decision decided [engine, when outcome=winner]. closed->tiebreak round open [engine, when outcome=tied and tiebreak_policy=revote and no revote has happened yet: a fresh Final over only the tied options, ballots cleared, 24h default]. closed->decided by coin [engine, when tied after the revote or policy=coin: seeded random with the seed and result stored; shown as an animation; logged 'Coin flip: Porto']. closed->needs_organizer [engine, when outcome=no_quorum, empty (Gather produced 0 ideas or everyone skipped), or tied with policy=organizer]. needs_organizer->open|decided [organizer picks one of: Extend, Decide with what we have (rule runs on the ballots present, quorum waived and logged), Pick myself (logged 'Dana decided: Thai, 2 votes in')]. open|closed->voided [organizer Reopen: spawns a fresh round of the same type and options, reason logged; organizer Cancel decision]. decided->active [organizer Reopen decision: spawns a new Final (same options, or edited); previous outcome stays visible as 'was Lisbon, Jun 2'; every downstream decided decision flips to stale with a 'check again' badge, never auto-reopened].

RULES. Gather: result = all non-withdrawn ideas; organizer may merge duplicates (merged option records its source). 1 idea -> engine skips straight to a Check ('Only one idea: Lisbon. Go with it?'). Shortlist: count approvals; options with 0 approvals are eliminated; sort desc; cut at N; ties at the cut all advance (shortlist may exceed N). If options <= N at open, the Shortlist round is skipped entirely ('already short enough'). If exactly 1 survives, decided directly ('everyone shortlisted only X'). Final with 2 options: strict majority of non-skipped ballots; tie -> tiebreak policy. Final with 3+ (ranked): instant runoff; winner needs >50% of continuing ballots; each step eliminates the option with fewest current votes; elimination tie broken by fewer approvals in this decision's Shortlist round, then by seeded random (seed stored); partial rankings allowed, exhausted ballots drop out; a ballot with no rankings = skip. If the last two tie -> tiebreak policy. Check: yes > no among non-skipped ballots and quorum met -> passed; tie = failed ('ties mean no'); failed -> decision back to planned with the proposal marked rejected.

QUORUM. eligible = event members with role voter or organizer at the moment of close (joining mid-round is allowed; you count from then). participated = submitted + skipped. quorum_rule per decision: majority (default: participated > eligible/2), everyone, or a fixed number. Checked only at close. Skip counts toward quorum and toward early close but toward no option. Non-response is not a skip.

DEADLINES. closes_at is required, in the event timezone; defaults by type: Gather 3 days, Shortlist 2 days, Final 2 days, Check 1 day, Quick vote 3 hours (or 'by 5pm' preset). Reminders to non-participants at T-24h and T-2h; 'last call: just you, Noa' when one person remains. Extend is unlimited but each extension is a ledger entry.

ORGANIZER. Any event member can be made organizer; multiple allowed. Every organizer action is a ledger entry visible to all ('Dana extended Shortlist to Tue', 'Dad took Tokyo off the table: too expensive'). Organizers vote like everyone else; there are no vote weights in MVP.

TIMELINE ATTACHMENT. Decisions have phase=before (ordered, with optional depends_on) or phase=during (pinned to a for_date within the event's date range). Trip template: Destination (G->S->F) -> Dates & length (G date ranges -> S 'I can make it' -> F) [depends on Destination] -> Lodging (G links -> S -> F) [depends on Dates] -> Getting there (Quick). During the trip the event page shows today's pinned quick votes at the top; a 'Tonight's dinner' quick vote is a Final with a 5pm close, early close on, quorum majority, tiebreak coin.

**Auth model.** Providers: Google, Apple, Facebook via OAuth (Auth.js), plus email magic link as the no-social fallback. No passwords, no phone numbers. We store name, avatar, email (for reminders) only. Invite flow: an event has an invite link (token, role voter|viewer, optional expiry and max uses) that the organizer pastes into Messenger; opening it shows 'Dana invited you to Summer '27 Trip, 5 people in, 2 decisions open' above the login buttons (this is the existing Login mockup); after login the member lands directly on the round that needs them. Joining an event also adds you to that family circle unless the organizer marked the invite guest-only. Kids: a parent creates a Kid profile (name, avatar, optional birth year, 4-digit PIN) under their account; the kid votes (a) via the profile switcher on the parent's phone (PIN required so siblings cannot vote for each other), or (b) on their own device via a parent-generated kid link/QR that binds that device to the kid profile only, revocable from the parent's settings. Kid ballots are attributed to the kid, weighted 1 like everyone else. Guests (v1): an invite can be 'guest' scope: they enter a name only, get an event-scoped identity that expires with the event, and can vote if the invite role is voter; they can upgrade to a social login later and keep their history. Viewers can see everything and never vote; organizers can flip a member between voter and viewer per event.

### Roundtable (event-as-ledger)

Angle: the event as the container.

Roundtable makes the event, not the poll, the thing you open: a trip, a party, or the standing Friday dinner is a container holding an ordered list of decisions, each settled by a short series of voting rounds. At the top of every event sits a living "Decided so far" ledger that the family can share back into Messenger as one link that stays current. Polls never float loose; every vote lands in a decision, every decision lands in the ledger, and afterwards the event becomes the family's record of what was chosen and why.

**Differentiator.** The Decided-So-Far ledger: an append-only, shareable record that answers "what have we settled, what is open, what is waiting on whom" for the whole event, with each entry carrying its outcome, the margin, the round history and the date. A Messenger poll answers one question and then scrolls away; Roundtable's ledger link (fampoll.app/s/<token>) is the same URL all summer, updates itself as rounds close, and renders as a card preview when pasted into the family thread. Dependencies (lodging waits for dates) make the event feel like a plan, not a pile of polls.

**Core loop.** 1) Organizer creates an event from a template (Trip seeds Destination, Dates and how long, Getting there, Where we stay, Day trips, in that order with dependencies) and sends one invite link into the Messenger thread. 2) The first unblocked decision is opened as a round (Ideas gathering or a Shortlist vote) with a deadline; members get a push or email and the link. 3) Each member votes in one tap (kids via avatar switch on a parent's phone); the round shows live who has voted and who is outstanding, and closes early when everyone has voted. 4) Organizer advances (top 2 to a Final), finalizes, reopens, or makes an organizer call; the decision flips to Decided and a ledger entry is written with outcome, margin and date. 5) The next dependent decision unlocks and appears in everyone's "Needs your vote" inbox; the shared summary link updates automatically and the organizer optionally posts the card back into Messenger. 6) After the event, the ledger becomes the recap (what we chose over what, with photos), and the sequence of decisions is saved as a household template for next year.

**Rounds model.** Decision states: blocked (a dependency is undecided) -> open (has an active or draft round) -> decided -> reopened (organizer only; writes a ledger revision, never deletes). Round states: draft -> open -> closed -> resolved (advanced | finalized | organizer_call). Round kinds and presets: Ideas (members add options, optional thumbs-up, no elimination), Shortlist (approval voting, pick up to N of M, top K advance, default N=2 K=2), Final (single choice among finalists), Quick (a one-round decision: single choice or approval, 3h default deadline). The default trip preset is Ideas -> Shortlist -> Final, but the organizer can skip straight to Final at any point. Who advances: the event organizer or a co-organizer taps 'Start the final', 'Finalize', 'Reopen' or 'Take X, done' (an organizer call, shown in the ledger as such). Auto-advance is a per-round toggle: 'when this closes, start the final with the top 2 automatically', default on for template-created rounds so the organizer is never the bottleneck. Deadlines: every round has one (defaults: 48h for trip rounds, 3h for quick votes, always shown in the voter's local time); a round also closes early when every member with voting rights has voted (default on). Quorum: default is more than half of voting members; if the deadline passes below quorum the round does not resolve and the organizer sees 'extend 24h / close anyway / nudge the missing'. Votes are changeable until close and are visible by name (family context; anonymous mode is a later per-event option). Ties: per-event policy, default 'organizer breaks the tie' with the choice logged; alternatives are a 24h tie-break round among tied options only, or a visible random draw. Kids' votes count by default; an event or decision can be set to 'kids advisory' (shown but excluded from the tally). Weights are otherwise 1 per person. Each resolution appends a LedgerEntry: outcome, margin ('beat Porto 5-1 in the final'), rounds used, decided_at and mode (vote or organizer call).

**Auth model.** Providers: Google, Apple and Facebook via OAuth, plus email magic link as fallback; no passwords ever. Facebook is kept despite app-review cost because the family already lives in Messenger; Apple is required for iOS. Invite flow: organizer creates an event and gets a short link (fampoll.app/j/<token>, 30-day expiry, revocable) to paste into the Messenger thread; opening it shows the login screen with context ('Dana invited you to Summer '27 Trip, 5 people in, 2 decisions open') and after sign-in the user is auto-joined to that event and dropped onto the first open round. First-time joiners from a household invite are added to the Household circle so later events can be shared with 'Family' in one tap; event-only invites add the person as a guest. Roles per event: organizer, co-organizer, voter, viewer. Kids: a parent creates a kid profile in the household (name, avatar, no email, no OAuth, no data collected beyond that) and the kid votes either by tapping their avatar on the parent's phone (the vote is recorded as cast_on_behalf_by the parent) or from a kid device through a PIN-protected link the parent generates; kids cannot create events, invite, or advance rounds. Guests: anyone who joins via an event invite without being in the household; membership is scoped to that event, they can propose ideas and vote but not advance rounds, and the organizer can flip them to viewer (a grandparent who only wants to follow). Sessions are long-lived (90 days) so Nana is not asked to log in twice a trip.

**MVP features.**

- Event container with ordered decisions: Requirement 1. A poll cannot exist outside a decision inside an event; this is the whole organizing idea.
- Decided-so-far ledger (per event, append-only): Requirement 2. The running record of outcomes with margin, date and round history; also the source for the shared summary.
- Round series per decision: Ideas -> Shortlist -> Final: Requirement 3. Presets so a normal user never configures rules; approval voting with pick-up-to-N, top-K advance, single-choice final.
- Quick vote (one-round decision): Friday dinner must be one tap; multi-round machinery has to be optional or the app dies on the common case.
- Deadlines with early close when everyone has voted: Rounds must end on their own so the organizer is never the bottleneck.

**Risks.**

**Patterns to borrow.**

- Rallly 'Finalize': closing a poll requires picking a winner, which locks the poll, stamps the outcome, and notifies everyone. Make this the only way a poll ends. The finalized outcome is the 'decision' record for requirement 2.
- Partiful 'Find a Time' write-back: the winning option writes into a field on the event (date poll -> trip dates; destination poll -> trip location; 'how long' poll -> duration). The event page then reads like a filled-in fact sheet of decisions, each linking to the poll that produced it.
- Splitwise group model: Event (trip/outing) is the top-level container with members, an activity feed (poll opened, votes cast, round closed, decision made), and pending members added by email/phone who can vote via link before they ever sign up.
- Loomio typed rounds and outcome statements: model a Question with Rounds, not a new poll per round. Round 1 = nominate/shortlist (multi-select or approval), Round 2 = ranked or single-choice among survivors, optional tie-break. Each round carries a one-line outcome. Ship exactly three round templates (Shortlist, Run-off, Tie-break) and hide the theory.
- Doodle grid: participants as rows, options as columns, cells as yes/maybe/no. Best-in-class at showing who is blocking what; use it for date and duration questions.
- Doodle 'if need be' / Partiful Yes-No-Maybe: three-state votes surface soft consensus and make run-offs unnecessary half the time. Default for date questions.
- iOS 26 'Add Choice': any member can add an option during the nomination round; creator can lock options when opening the final round.
- WhatsApp Aug 2026 poll controls: per-poll deadline, optional hide-voter-names toggle, and a short edit window after creation. Copy all three. Add auto-close at deadline with auto-finalize when there is a clear winner and auto-run-off when tied.
- Troupe nudges: deadline reminders sent only to members who have not voted, plus a one-tap 'poke' the organizer can send. Batch vote notifications into a digest instead of WhatsApp-style per-vote pings.
- Troupe ranked-choice for the final round of destination/restaurant picks (instant run-off computed automatically instead of asking people to vote again).
- Wanderlog likes: allow thumbs-up on options before a poll is formally opened, as a cheap signal for what deserves to reach the shortlist.
- Rallly/Partiful/Doodle account-free voting: voters open a link, tap their name (or type it once), vote. Identity is claimed later by signing in with the same email/phone. Only the organizer needs a real account. This is what makes 'easy login' actually easy for a 70-year-old.
- Apple Invites co-hosts: allow more than one organizer per event so both parents can open polls and finalize.
- Partiful single share link plus text blast: one URL per event that works in Messenger, WhatsApp, and SMS previews; one 'send update' action that posts a summary to the group's chosen channel.
- Rallly per-poll comments: a thread under each question for the 'but the kids hate sushi' context, so it stays with the decision instead of in chat.
- Decision log view: a chronological, filterable list of every finalized outcome across all events ('Decided: 5 nights, Lisbon, leaving Aug 3'). No competitor has this; it is the founder's requirement 2 and the retention hook.

**Patterns to avoid.**

- Poll-as-message (Messenger, WhatsApp, iMessage, Signal): no container, scrolls away, no history. FamPoll must be event-first, poll-second.
- Immutable polls that force unsend-and-recreate (Messenger). Allow edits until the first vote, then a short edit window, then lock.
- Platform lock-in for voters (iOS 26 polls need every member on iOS 26; Troupe needs the app installed; Messenger needs Facebook). Be web-first with a link that works in any browser; native wrappers later.
- Doodle's 2026 paywall shape: one active poll, ads, yes/maybe/no and deadlines behind Pro. Families run a handful of polls a month; never meter core voting. If you charge, charge organizers for something adjacent (archives, more events, SMS blasts).
- Account walls for voters (Doodle now requires an account to create; many trip apps require install to vote). Require sign-in only to create or finalize.
- Scope creep into itineraries, budgets, packing lists, booking (Wanderlog, WePlanify, SquadTrip). Decide, record, hand off via links to Maps/TripIt/Splitwise. Do not become a travel app; half the founder's polls are 'where to eat tonight'.
- Governance vocabulary (Loomio's consent/consensus/advice process). Rounds should be presented as 'Narrow it down' and 'Pick the winner', not as process templates.
- Voting-method zoo (StrawPoll's five methods plus probability voting). Ship yes/maybe/no, pick-one, pick-many, and ranked (final round only). Nothing else in v1.
- Anonymous-by-default. Families want to see who voted for what (iMessage and Signal get this right); default visible, allow per-poll hiding for touchy topics.
- Per-vote push notifications to the creator (WhatsApp). Digest and threshold notifications only: 'everyone has voted', 'deadline in 2h, 3 people missing', 'round closed'.
- A new poll object for every round (what Troupe and Doodle force). Rounds must be versions of one question or the decision history becomes unreadable.
- Ending a poll without an outcome (Doodle, StrawPoll, chat polls simply stop). Every close produces a finalized decision or an explicit 'no decision, reopen' state.
- Facebook-only or Facebook-first identity. Meta's login is the most administratively expensive provider (Live mode, privacy policy, data-deletion endpoint, Advanced Access, likely Business Verification) and breaks for anyone who has left Facebook.
- Collecting emails or accounts from kids to let them vote. Give minors name-only guest votes inside the family event; no COPPA exposure.

**Social login notes.** Opinionated recommendation for an indie app in Sept 2026: ship web-first with (1) email magic link / 6-digit code, (2) Google, (3) Sign in with Apple for organizers; voters use a per-event invite link with name-only guest identity that can be claimed later by signing in with a matching email. Add passkeys as a post-login upgrade prompt in v1.1. Defer Facebook Login unless the family literally has no Google/Apple identities. Use a hosted auth provider rather than hand-rolling: Supabase Auth (50k MAU free, Google/Apple/Facebook/magic link/passkeys, open-source GoTrue) or Clerk (free tier raised to 50k monthly retained users on Feb 5, 2026, best prebuilt UI, expensive past the free tier); Better Auth if you want to self-host on Next.js. All three handle the Apple client-secret JWT for you.

GOOGLE: Sign-in with only openid/email/profile uses non-sensitive scopes, so the full OAuth app-verification audit is not required; you can publish the consent screen to Production and serve unlimited users. In Testing mode an External-audience app is capped at 100 listed test users. Do brand verification (verified domain, homepage, privacy policy, logo) so the consent screen shows your name/logo instead of the raw client id; without it users see a less trustworthy prompt and revoke more. Do NOT request Calendar or Contacts scopes at login: those are sensitive and trigger verification (Contacts/Gmail-class scopes are restricted and require a paid security assessment). Google One Tap on web is the highest-converting returning-login pattern.

APPLE: App Store Guideline 4.8 (fetched verbatim today): if your iOS app uses a third-party or social login (Facebook, Google, X, LinkedIn, Amazon, WeChat) for the primary account, you must also offer an equivalent login that limits data to name and email, lets users keep their email private, and does not collect interactions for advertising without consent. Sign in with Apple is not named as mandatory, but it is the only mainstream provider that satisfies the 'keep email private' clause, so in practice: native iOS app + Google/Facebook login = add Sign in with Apple. Exemption: if the app uses exclusively your own account system (magic link / passkeys only), 4.8 does not apply. A web app or PWA is outside App Review entirely. Costs and gotchas: Apple Developer Program $99/year; web needs a Services ID with domain and return-URL registration; the client secret is a JWT you sign with a .p8 key and it cannot expire more than 6 months out, so rotate it or generate per request; Apple returns the user's name only on the first authorization (store it immediately or you lose it); users who choose Hide My Email get a privaterelay.appleid.com address that you can only email from a sender domain registered with Apple, which breaks magic-link fallback and email-based account merging for those users.

FACEBOOK: Attractive because the family is on Messenger, but heaviest to operate. public_profile and email are auto-granted at Standard Access, which only works for people who have a role on your app; serving the general public requires Advanced Access, and Meta's docs state Business Verification is required for apps requesting Advanced Access (I could not reach developers.facebook.com to confirm whether the Consumer app type still exempts these two permissions, treat it as likely required and needing a legal business entity and documents). The app must be in Live mode with a privacy policy URL and a Data Deletion Callback URL or instructions page. On iOS, users who decline ATT tracking get Limited Login (OIDC token, no Graph API). News as of Aug 27, 2026: Meta put Login with Facebook into open beta with one-tap returning login on Android and web, silent token refresh for Limited Login on iOS, and routing iOS logins through the native app. Verdict: nice-to-have for v2.

MAGIC LINK / OTP: Cheapest path to 'easy login'; Resend or Postmark, transactional domain with SPF/DKIM/DMARC. Send a link and a 6-digit code (links open in a different browser than the app half the time on mobile). Long sessions (90 days) so a family member signs in roughly once a year. Skip SMS OTP: carrier cost, and FBI/CISA guidance in 2025 argued against SMS-only auth.

PASSKEYS: FIDO Alliance 2026 numbers (via search): ~5B passkeys in use, 75% of consumers have one on at least one account. Mature enough to offer, not mature enough to be the only path for grandparents. Offer 'sign in faster next time' after the first magic-link or social login; keep magic link as recovery.

ACCOUNT MODEL: key users by verified email, merge Google and Apple identities that share an email, allow manual linking for Apple relay addresses, and let a name-only guest voter claim their votes when they later sign in from the same invite. Minors: guest votes inside a family event, no email collected.

## Gap check on the first draft

The brief is strong on identity (seats, not accounts), on the two-round vocabulary, and on the record artifacts (decided-so-far header, ledger, versioned /s/ page, Copy for Messenger), and its reading of Apple guideline 4.8 is correct for a web app. It is not yet sound as a spec to build from. Three things break on paper: Reopen copies the old ballots into a round that closes early once everyone has voted, so Undo re-decides the same thing instantly; the "one INSERT ... ON CONFLICT" vote path and the votes table as drawn cannot hold a pick-up-to-2 ballot with toggles and Skip, and the everyone-voted early close races under READ COMMITTED; and the "one link" promise is actually three links (family claim link, personal link, public /s/ summary) whose relationship the core loop never settles. Four more are important: "frozen ballot" contradicts "add an option during round 1"; quorum only guards the majority auto-decide, so a 1-of-6 turnout narrows the field for everyone and all-skip/zero-ballot closes have no defined outcome; the "step 1 or 2" round model does not hold once Final round and Narrow down can chain; and the screen inventory is missing event creation, per-event roster pinning (the very denominator "everyone" depends on), open-round organizer actions, and Undo. Requirement 4 has been quietly reinterpreted (one organizer, Google only, Facebook deferred) without asking the founder. On buildability: the repo is not greenfield, but the scaffold's identity model is the opposite of the brief's (every voter is a Clerk user; /join is Clerk-protected; votes.cast_by_user_id is NOT NULL to users; the engine has ideas/shortlist/final with plurality-wins finals and no quorum, skip, majority, coin, or attempts), so "keep the scaffold plus five schema changes" is really a rewrite of auth, actions, queries, lifecycle, the engine, and most screens, with screens 1 and 9 undesigned. Four weekends is plausible only with the cuts listed below; six is the honest number as written.
